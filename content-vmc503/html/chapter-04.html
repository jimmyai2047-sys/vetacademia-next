Chapter 4 - Nomenclature and Classification of Viruses

LEARNING OBJECTIVES

After studying this chapter, the student will be able to:

  • Trace the development of virus classification from host- and disease-based groupings to modern genome-based taxonomy.
  • Describe the criteria used to classify viruses and the role of the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV).
  • Explain the 15-rank ICTV hierarchy, virus realms and the binomial format of virus species names, and distinguish a virus from a virus species.
  • Describe the Baltimore classification and assign important animal virus families to Baltimore classes.
  • Apply correct nomenclature for viruses, strains, serotypes, genotypes and lineages, including influenza and FMD strain designations.

LECTURE 5: PRINCIPLES, HISTORY AND THE ICTV SYSTEM

4.1 Why Classify Viruses?

Classification (taxonomy) orders the enormous diversity of viruses into groups that share common properties and evolutionary history, while nomenclature provides names for these groups. A sound classification allows virologists to predict properties of a newly discovered virus (e.g. its replication strategy, stability, antiviral susceptibility and likely diseases), aids diagnosis, epidemiological communication, regulation (biosafety, trade, vaccine licensing) and the study of viral evolution.

Today, phylogenetic analysis of genome sequences (particularly conserved genes such as RdRp, DNA polymerase, helicase and major capsid protein) is the principal basis for classification at all ranks, supplemented by the other properties. Species demarcation criteria vary by family (e.g. percentage sequence identity thresholds, host range, antigenicity).

4.4 The International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV)

  • The ICTV is the only body authorised by the International Union of Microbiological Societies (IUMS) to develop, refine and maintain a universal virus taxonomy. Founded in 1966 as the International Committee on Nomenclature of Viruses (ICNV) and renamed ICTV in the 1970s.
  • Taxonomic changes are proposed as taxonomic proposals (TaxoProps), reviewed by study groups and subcommittees (animal DNA viruses and retroviruses; animal dsRNA and ssRNA− viruses; animal ssRNA+ viruses; bacterial and archaeal viruses; fungal and protist viruses; plant viruses), approved by the Executive Committee and ratified by the ICTV membership.
  • Approved taxonomy is published annually in the Master Species List (MSL) and the online ICTV Report on Virus Taxonomy (ictv.global), with "Virus Metadata Resource" linking species to exemplar isolates and GenBank sequences.
  • Scale of current taxonomy: the 2025-2026 ICTV taxonomy release (MSL41) recognises 10 realms, 11 kingdoms, 22 phyla, 50 classes, 105 orders, 427 families, 232 subfamilies, 4,149 genera and 17,554 species — and the numbers grow every year as metagenomic discoveries are classified.
  • The International Code of Virus Classification and Nomenclature (ICVCN) lays down the rules; it governs names of taxa (from species to realm) but not the names of viruses themselves, which are not regulated by ICTV.

4.5 The ICTV Hierarchy and Nomenclature Rules

4.5.1 The 15 Ranks

In 2019–2020, ICTV expanded the classification from the traditional five ranks (order, family, subfamily, genus, species) to a 15-rank hierarchy (Gorbalenya et al., 2020), comparable to the Linnaean system used for cellular organisms. Eight principal ranks and seven derived ranks are recognised.

4.5.2 Rules for Writing Names

  • Names of taxa (realm to genus and species) are written in italics with the first letter capitalised (e.g. Paramyxoviridae, Morbillivirus). When a taxon name is used formally, the rank may precede it: "the family Poxviridae".
  • Binomial species names: since 2021, ICTV has required all virus species to have Linnaean- style binomial names consisting of the genus name followed by a species epithet (e.g. Betacoronavirus pandemicum, Orthoflavivirus japonicum). The renaming of existing species was largely completed during 2021–2024. The entire binomial is italicised; the epithet begins with a lower-case letter (or may be alphanumeric).
  • Viruses are not species: a species is an abstract taxonomic category (a concept), whereas a virus is a physical entity that is assigned to a species. Virus names are not italicised and not capitalised (except proper nouns): "rabies virus", "Newcastle disease virus", "severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2)". Thus Betacoronavirus pandemicum is the species; SARS-CoV-2 and SARS-CoV are viruses belonging to it.
  • Abbreviations of virus names are widely used (FMDV, NDV, PPRV, BTV, LSDV) but are not official taxa.
  • Informal and vernacular names such as "herpesviruses", "poxviruses", "arboviruses" and "coronaviruses" are written in plain type without capitals.
  • Below species (not regulated by ICTV): genotype, serotype, lineage/clade, topotype, strain, isolate, variant, pathotype (e.g. velogenic NDV) and biotype (e.g. cytopathic and non- cytopathic BVDV) — Section 4.9.

4.5.3 Current Realms

  • A realm groups viruses believed to share a common origin, identified by hallmark genes or protein folds. The MSL41 recognises 10 realms. Those containing animal viruses are:

◦ Riboviria — all RNA viruses that encode an RNA-dependent RNA polymerase (kingdom Orthornavirae) and viruses encoding a reverse transcriptase (kingdom Pararnavirae — retroviruses and hepadnaviruses).

◦ Duplodnaviria — dsDNA viruses with the HK97-fold major capsid protein — tailed bacteriophages (Caudoviricetes) and herpesviruses (order Herpesvirales).

◦ Varidnaviria — dsDNA viruses with double (or single) jelly-roll capsid proteins — adenoviruses, poxviruses, asfarviruses (ASFV), iridoviruses and giant viruses (Nucleocytoviricota).

◦ Monodnaviria — small ssDNA viruses with rolling-circle replication initiation proteins and their descendants — parvoviruses, circoviruses, and the small dsDNA papillomaviruses and polyomaviruses.

◦ Ribozyviria — hepatitis delta virus-like agents.

  • Other realms (e.g. Adnaviria and the newly created Singelaviria, 2025) contain viruses of archaea, bacteria and protists. Some families (e.g. Anelloviridae) remain unassigned to a realm.

LECTURE 6: BALTIMORE CLASSIFICATION, ANIMAL VIRUS FAMILIES AND BELOW-SPECIES NOMENCLATURE

4.6 The Baltimore Classification

In 1971, David Baltimore proposed that all viruses must produce messenger RNA (mRNA) that can be translated by host ribosomes, and that viruses could be grouped according to the pathway from genome to mRNA. By convention, mRNA is designated positive (+) sense; nucleic acid complementary to mRNA is negative (−) sense. The system, later expanded to seven classes, is not a formal taxonomy but is an extremely useful framework for understanding replication strategies (Chapters 8 and 9) and for predicting which enzymes a virus must carry or encode.

4.9.1 Strain Designation Conventions

  • Influenza viruses (WHO convention): type / host (omitted if human) / geographic origin / strain number / year of isolation (subtype) — e.g. A/chicken/India/10CA01/2021(H5N1) or A/swine/Iowa/15/1930(H1N1).
  • FMD viruses: serotype / country code / isolate number / year — e.g. O/IND/R2/1975 (Indian vaccine strain), with topotype and lineage defined by VP1 sequencing (e.g. O/ME-SA/Ind- 2001e).
  • Rotaviruses: group / species of origin / country / strain / year / G and P genotypes — e.g. RVA/Cow-wt/IND/B-11/2011/G10P[11].
  • Sequence-based names are linked to GenBank accession numbers; metagenomically discovered viruses are often named by location or host (e.g. "Kasba virus", "Umbre virus").

INDIA FOCUS: VIRUSES NAMED FROM INDIA AND INDIAN NOMENCLATURE IN PRACTICE

  • Viruses discovered and named in India by ICMR–NIV Pune and collaborators include Kyasanur Forest disease virus (Shimoga district, Karnataka, 1957; Orthoflavivirus), Chandipura virus (Chandipur village, Maharashtra, 1965; Vesiculovirus, Rhabdoviridae), Ganjam virus (Odisha; the Asian variant of Nairobi sheep disease virus, Nairoviridae), Bhanja virus (Odisha; Phenuiviridae) and several other arboviruses named after Indian localities (e.g. Kasba, Umbre, Sathuperi, Chittoor viruses).
  • Serotypes and lineages in the national FMD programme: India has three FMDV serotypes — O, A and Asia 1 (serotype C has not been reported since 1995). Serotype O viruses belong to the ME-SA topotype, with successive dominant lineages (e.g. Ind-2001, with sublineages Ind-2001d and Ind- 2001e) identified by VP1 sequencing at ICAR–National Institute on Foot-and-Mouth Disease (NIFMD), Bhubaneswar, which also guides vaccine-strain matching (e.g. O/IND/R2/1975, A/IND/40/2000, Asia 1/IND/63/1972).
  • Bluetongue: India is one of the most BTV-diverse countries, with more than 20 serotypes reported; the indigenous inactivated pentavalent/multivalent BTV vaccine (ICAR) is based on locally prevalent serotypes, illustrating why serotype nomenclature matters for vaccine design.
  • Other examples: NDV genotype XIII viruses predominate in Indian poultry outbreaks; Indian PPRV isolates belong to lineage IV, and the homologous vaccine strain Sungri/96 is widely used in India; H5N1 viruses in Indian poultry have belonged mainly to clades 2.2 (2006–2008) and 2.3.2.1a (predominant in later outbreaks), while clade 2.3.4.4b viruses have spread widely across Asia.
  • Correct use of binomial species names and strain designations is now expected in Indian theses and journals, and GenBank submissions from Indian laboratories should follow ICTV and WHO/WOAH conventions.

4.10 Summary

  • Early classifications by host, disease or transmission are useful but not taxonomic; the LHT system (1962) introduced classification by virion properties.
  • The ICTV (founded 1966) is the official body for virus taxonomy; the MSL41 (2026) recognises 10 realms, 427 families, 4,149 genera and 17,554 species.
  • Classification uses genome, morphology, physicochemical, protein, replication, antigenic and biological properties, with genome phylogeny now central.
  • ICTV uses 15 ranks from realm (-viria) to species; since 2021 species have binomial names (e.g. Lyssavirus rabies, Betacoronavirus pandemicum).
  • Species are italicised taxa; viruses (rabies virus, SARS-CoV-2) are physical entities, written in plain type and not regulated by ICTV.
  • The Baltimore classification groups viruses into seven classes according to the route from genome to mRNA.
  • Below-species categories — serotype, subtype, genotype, lineage, topotype, pathotype, biotype, strain, isolate and variant — are important in diagnosis, epidemiology and vaccine selection; influenza and FMD viruses have standard strain designations.
  • India has contributed virus names such as KFD, Chandipura and Ganjam viruses, and uses serotype/lineage nomenclature for FMD, BTV, NDV, PPRV and avian influenza control.
